What a book editor actually does for you

Short answer: a book editor takes a finished draft and makes it readable, consistent and credible without changing who wrote it. I work in three passes — developmental editing fixes the structure and argument, line and copy editing fixes the sentences, grammar and consistency, and proofreading catches the last errors before the file goes to print.

For Christian nonfiction there is a fourth job that general editors miss: guarding doctrine and Scripture. I check every quoted verse against the translation you named, keep your citation style consistent, flag places where a claim needs support, and tell you honestly when a chapter preaches instead of writes.

My job is to protect your message and your voice while fixing everything around it.

The draft is done, but it rambles

Chapters repeat, the testimony arrives too late, and readers drift by chapter three.

It reads like a sermon, not a book

Preaching works from a pulpit. On the page it needs a different rhythm — I rebuild it without losing the fire.

Scripture is inconsistent

Three translations in one chapter, unmarked paraphrases, and references that do not match the verse.

KDP rejected the file

Front matter, headings, page numbers and trim size all have rules. I prepare the manuscript so formatting goes smoothly.

Editing services

Not sure which one you need? Send the manuscript. I read it and tell you — and I will say so if you only need the cheaper pass.

i

Developmental editing

The big-picture pass. I look at structure, argument, chapter order, pacing, theology and whether the book keeps the promise its title makes. You get a written editorial report plus in-manuscript notes on what to cut, move, expand or rethink.

  • Editorial report
  • Chapter restructure
  • Argument & flow
  • Reader clarity
Best forFinished first drafts, sermon series being turned into a book, and memoirs that are all true but not yet in the right order.
ii

Line editing

Sentence by sentence. I cut padding, fix rhythm, break up sermon-length paragraphs, strengthen weak verbs and make transitions land — all in Word with tracked changes, so you approve or reject every single edit.

  • Tracked changes
  • Voice preserved
  • Tone & rhythm
  • Margin comments
Best forManuscripts where the structure is already right but the prose feels heavy, repetitive or stiff.
iii

Copy editing

Grammar, punctuation, spelling, tense, capitalisation of divine pronouns, Scripture references, quotation formatting and a style sheet so every choice stays consistent from page one to the end. Chicago Manual of Style by default; I follow your publisher's guide if you have one.

  • Style sheet
  • Scripture checked
  • CMOS
  • US or UK English
Best forBooks heading to KDP or IngramSpark that need to look professionally produced.
iv

Proofreading

The final read before publication. Typos, double spaces, broken headings, wrong page references, stray formatting — the small errors that make a good book look self-published in the bad way.

  • Final pass
  • Front & back matter
  • Print-ready check
Best forBooks already edited and laid out, one step from upload.
v

Publishing prep for KDP & IngramSpark

Front matter and back matter built properly — title page, copyright page, dedication, foreword, about the author, ministry links and a call to action that actually converts readers into your community. Plus book description and keyword help so people can find the book once it is live.

  • Front & back matter
  • Book blurb
  • Amazon keywords
  • Metadata
Best forSelf-publishing authors who do not want to learn Amazon's rules the hard way.

Getting started

What kind of books do you edit?

Christian nonfiction and general nonfiction: memoirs and testimonies, devotionals, Bible studies, theology and apologetics, sermon-to-book projects, Christian self-help, personal development, leadership, marriage and family books. I do not edit fiction, academic dissertations or technical manuals, and I will refer you to someone who does rather than take work I cannot do well.

Do I need a developmental edit, a line edit, or just proofreading?

If you are unsure whether the book works at all — whether the chapters are in the right order, whether readers will follow the argument — you need a developmental edit. If the structure is right but the writing feels heavy, repetitive or preachy, you need a line edit. If the writing is already strong and you want grammar, Scripture references and consistency handled, you need a copy edit. If the book is finished and formatted and you want a last safety net before upload, you need proofreading.

Send the manuscript and I will tell you which one you actually need. I regularly tell authors they need less than they asked for.

Can I see your work before I pay?

Yes. I edit 1,000 words of your manuscript free, with tracked changes and comments, before any money changes hands. It is the fastest way for both of us to know whether we are a good fit.

My manuscript is not finished yet. Should I still contact you?

Yes, especially if you are stuck. A short structural consultation on an unfinished draft can save you months of writing in the wrong direction. That said, a full developmental edit works best on a complete draft, however rough it is.

What file format do you need?

Microsoft Word (.docx) is best, because tracked changes and comments work properly there. I can also work from a Google Doc. PDFs are difficult to edit, so if that is all you have, tell me and we will sort it out.

Price, time and payment

How much does editing cost?

Price depends on three things: the length of the manuscript, the level of editing it needs, and how soon you need it back. I quote a fixed price for the whole project after reading a sample, so there are no surprises and no meter running. Hourly work starts at $10 per hour through Upwork.

Send your word count and your deadline and you will have a quote, usually within 24 hours.

How long will my book take?

As a rough guide: a copy edit or proofread of a 50,000-word manuscript takes about one to two weeks. A line edit takes two to three. A developmental edit takes three to four, because it involves reading the whole book before touching a word. You get an exact delivery date in writing before we start, and I deliver longer books in batches so you can begin reviewing while I work.

Do you offer a rush service?

Sometimes, depending on my schedule. Rush work carries a surcharge because it means moving other authors. Ask, and I will tell you honestly whether I can do it well in the time you have. I would rather decline than deliver a rushed edit with your name on it.

How do I pay, and is it safe?

Most clients hire me through Upwork, where payment is held in escrow and released when you are satisfied — that protects you as much as it protects me. For direct clients I usually take 50% to start and 50% on delivery, by bank transfer or an agreed international method. Terms are always written down before work begins.

What if I am not happy with the edit?

Tell me specifically what is not working and I will fix it. One round of revisions is included in every project. Because every edit is delivered in tracked changes, you can also reject anything you disagree with yourself, line by line. And because you see a free sample first, real mismatches almost never get that far.

How the editing works

Will you change my voice?

No. Protecting your voice is the whole job. I cut what gets in the way of it — padding, repetition, tangled sentences — so what remains sounds more like you, not less. Where a change is significant, I leave a comment explaining why, and you decide.

How do you handle Scripture and doctrine?

We agree on your Bible translation before I start, and I check every quoted verse against it word for word. Citations are formatted consistently, paraphrases are marked as paraphrases, and divine pronoun capitalisation follows the style you choose and stays that way throughout.

On doctrine, I do not impose my own position. If a statement seems unsupported or could be misread, I raise it as a margin question so you can decide. Your theology stays yours.

What style guide do you use?

The Chicago Manual of Style by default, which is the standard for trade nonfiction, alongside Merriam-Webster for spelling. If your publisher uses another guide, I follow theirs. Every project comes with a style sheet recording the choices made, which matters if you write a second book.

Can you edit in British English?

Yes. We set US or UK English at the start and I keep it consistent throughout — spelling, punctuation, quotation marks and date formats.

English is my second language. Will you be able to work with my draft?

Absolutely, and I do it often. A strong message written in imperfect English is far easier to fix than a weak message written fluently. I will smooth the grammar and phrasing while keeping the cadence that makes your writing yours.

Do you use AI to edit my book?

No. Your manuscript is read and edited by me. I may use standard spelling and consistency checkers the way every professional editor does, but judgement calls about your argument, your voice and your theology are human ones, and they are mine.

Can you write or ghostwrite the book for me?

My core service is editing what you have written. For authors who have the message but not the pages, I can discuss developmental coaching or ghostwriting support separately — message me with what you have and what you want the finished book to be.

Publishing, rights and logistics

Will you help me publish on Amazon KDP or IngramSpark?

I prepare the manuscript so publishing goes smoothly: correctly ordered front and back matter, consistent heading structure, a copyright page, an author bio, and a book description with keywords that help readers find you. I am not a cover designer or an interior formatter, but I will point you to the right person and make sure what you hand them is clean.

Is my manuscript kept confidential?

Yes. I do not share, quote or publish any part of your manuscript, and I will sign a non-disclosure agreement if you want one. Unpublished work stays private, always.

Do you take any rights or royalties in my book?

None. You own your book completely — copyright, royalties and all decisions. I am a service provider, not a publisher or a partner. I do ask that you let me know when it launches, because I like seeing them go out.

You are in Nigeria and I am not. Does that matter?

Not at all. I work with authors across the US, UK, Canada and Africa. Lagos is GMT+1, which overlaps comfortably with both European and American working hours, and we communicate by email, WhatsApp or Upwork messages, plus video calls when they help.

How quickly do you reply?

Usually within a few hours during the working day, and within 24 hours otherwise. WhatsApp is the fastest way to reach me.
